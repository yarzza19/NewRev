# NewRev
NewRev is a non-profit platform dedicated to preserving automotive heritage by creating a digital library of discontinued vehicle parts. It uses 3D scanning, reverse engineering, CAD reconstruction and additive manufacturing to produce functional replacements for classic and obsolete cars.

My name is Pablo Yarza, and I am a Design Engineer with a Master's degree in Artificial Intelligence. I am the creator of NewRev, a non-profit initiative focused on preserving automotive heritage through digital manufacturing and open access to discontinued vehicle parts.

NewRev was originally developed as my Bachelor's Final Degree Project, where it received the highest academic distinction (Honors / Matrícula de Honor). The project has already been validated through practical manufacturing tests and technical review by AIDIMME, one of Spain's leading technology institutes specializing in industrial innovation and advanced manufacturing.

The purpose of NewRev is to create a digital library of discontinued automotive parts, allowing owners, restorers, workshops, museums, and enthusiast communities to reproduce components that are no longer available on the market. Today, the availability of classic vehicle parts follows a simple supply-and-demand dynamic: as parts become rarer, prices increase dramatically. In some cases, unique components such as cylinder heads, engine parts, or vehicle-specific assemblies can become prohibitively expensive or impossible to obtain.

As time passes, this problem will only worsen. Every year, more original components disappear, making it increasingly difficult to acquire examples for preservation and 3D scanning. NewRev aims to address this challenge by creating a long-term digital archive before these parts are permanently lost.

The project relies on 3D scanning, reverse engineering, CAD reconstruction, and industrial additive manufacturing technologies such as MJF, SLS, and metal SLM. The objective is not simply to replicate parts visually, but to manufacture functional replacements that meet engineering tolerances, dimensional accuracy requirements, and mechanical performance standards suitable for real-world use.

As part of the validation process, several components were successfully manufactured, including a full-scale 1:1 replacement part that was installed in the engine of a 1960s Biscúter microcar. The original components used for scanning were obtained through collaboration with the National Biscúter Club of Spain. This demonstrated that the methodology could be applied to real historic vehicles and not only theoretical prototypes.

The project is already available on GitHub and is intended to evolve into a community-driven platform that helps preserve vehicles that would otherwise become impossible to maintain. While the platform may charge symbolic fees to cover infrastructure, scanning, storage, and operational costs, NewRev's mission is fundamentally preservation-oriented rather than profit-driven.
